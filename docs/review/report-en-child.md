# What I thought of the AI guide — by Maya, age 9 (Year 5)

> Reviewer note: this report was returned in the reviewer's final message rather than
> written to disk directly, and is reproduced here verbatim.

## How it felt overall

The start was good. The eight friends made me want to keep going, and I liked that Katerina is 9.5 like me. But by about chapter 4 I was tired, because a lot of it is short lines with no full stops that just list stuff at me — like a shopping list of clever words. Chapters 7, 8, 9 and 14 felt like the same four boxes over and over (cancer, food waste, translating, climate) and I started scrolling past them. Some bits were far too grown-up ("non-negotiable", "CVs", "UI/UX") and nobody told me what they meant. Some bits confused me in a way that made me feel stupid, like chapter 5 saying AI CAN read X-rays and two lines later saying it CAN'T. The glossary was a let-down: it explained the easy words and left out every hard one I got stuck on. The final quiz was too easy — the longest or the first answer is nearly always right, so I could get 10/10 without reading anything.

## Findings, worst first

**1. Chapter 5 contradicts itself word-for-word.** "Detect illness from x-rays and MRI scans" appears in *both* "What can AI do?" and "What can AI not do?". I read it three times, then stopped believing either list. It also clashes with ch7 ("Spots cancer in X-rays"), ch9 ("Detects cancer early") and the glossary MRI entry. → Remove from the "can't" list, or say "read an X-ray *without a doctor checking it*".

**2. An unfinished bit is visible to me.** Ch6: "Prof. Ling — Hong Kong · AI & Finance / Placeholder: swap in Prof. Ling's real focus area and story here." I thought I'd broken the site, and it made me doubt whether the other professors are real.

**3. Ling is a 12-year-old kid AND a professor.** Intro: "Ling / Age 12, science geek & reader". Ch5: "Hello, I'm Professor Ling." Ch6: "Prof. Ling — Hong Kong". Ch11: "Hi, I'm Ling". I thought she'd grown up in the middle of the book.

**4. The final quiz can be beaten without reading.** In every question the right answer is first on the list or the longest, usually both (six of ten are first; the rest are longest). The wrong ones are silly on purpose — "Photoshop", "Excel", "Cook food", "Fly planes", "No one". I noticed by Q4 and stopped thinking. → Shuffle positions, make the wrong answers nearly-right and the same length.

**5. The quiz only covers a few chapters.** "see how much you've learned!" — but nothing from ch3 (machine learning), ch12 (careers), ch13 or ch14. Two questions (deepfake, MRI) are copied word-for-word from the glossary, so it tests the glossary, not the book.

**6. The glossary defines the easy words and skips the hard ones.** It promises "definitions for all the AI words you've met" and gives eleven, including "Aviation: The science of flying planes" and "Defence: Keeping people and countries safe" — I already knew those. Missing: hallucination, data, model, train, deploy, parameters, neural network, deep learning, machine learning, bias, binary, CPU, RAM, ethics, transparency, Human-in-the-Loop, CV, UI/UX, Gartner Hype Cycle. "Hallucination" is in the final quiz and isn't in the glossary.

**7. Some definitions are harder than the word.** "Humanities: Subjects like history, ethics and philosophy that help us understand people, society and right from wrong" — now I'm stuck on four words instead of one. "Food Engineer: … so more people can be fed sustainably" — "sustainably", and "can be fed" has nobody doing the feeding. "Artificial Intelligence: A type of intelligence created by humans" uses "intelligence" to explain "intelligence".

**8. "non-negotiable" (ch15).** "This is exactly why human oversight of AI is non-negotiable." I thought "oversight" meant a mistake. The most important sentence of the last chapter is the one I understood least.

**9. Ch4's heading is a 21-word sentence** with no full stop: "Artificial intelligence is a type of intelligence created by humans in the digital world to help automate tasks in the physical environment." I lost the thread halfway, and the chapter isn't even about that — it's about computer parts.

**10. Ch1's table is labels, not sentences.** "Physical Brain : a human processor of ideas, thoughts and knowledge…" / "Digital Computer : a digital processor of data to help us make sense of complex numbers". "Processor" I only know from *food* processor, so I pictured my brain chopping carrots. "Complex numbers" made me think hard maths was coming. And "Physical world" / "Digital world" are empty headings with everything jumbled in one list underneath, so I couldn't tell which side anything belonged to. This is the very first thing I read and the hardest thing in the book.

**11. "physical environment" sent me the wrong way.** At school "the environment" means trees and recycling, so for two chapters I thought AI was about saving the planet.

**12. Two different lists of how AI learns.** Ch2's five steps ("Feed it LOTS of data / It finds patterns / It makes guesses / We correct mistakes / It gets smarter!") then ch3's six completely different ones, with no link between them. And ch3 is called "How AI *Actually* Learns", which made me think ch2 had lied.

**13. Ch3's steps use locked words.** "Train Model" — I thought of a model aeroplane, so it sounded like teaching a doll. "Deploy! — Release it for real-world use" — never seen "deploy" in my life; I skipped it.

**14. The neural network bit says nothing I can picture.** "INPUT LAYER → HIDDEN LAYERS → OUTPUT LAYER. More layers = deeper learning = 'DEEP LEARNING'". Hidden from who? Are they secret? The equals signs look like a sum I should solve. "Neurons" isn't explained either.

**15. The GPT-4 question asks about a word explained afterwards.** I must pick between 1.8 million/billion/trillion "parameters" *before* being told a parameter is a little dial. Pure guess. And "so we're catching up fast!" — I read "we" as humans and got confused about who's catching up to whom.

**16. Ch7/8/9/14 repeat.** Ch14 is ch8 again with "in the future": Fashion, Art & Music and Food all appear in both, with the same headings. Cancer/medicine appears in ch5, 6, 7, 9 and 14. Food waste in 7, 9, 12 and 14. Translation in 6, 8, 9 and 13. By ch14 I was reading only the headings.

**17. Sentences with nobody doing anything.** Ch10: "Fairness must be designed in" — designed in to what, by whom? "Facial recognition in public" isn't a sentence; I can't tell if it's good or bad. "Trained on biased data" — "biased" is never explained anywhere, though it also appears in ch6 ("bugs and biases") and ch15.

**18. The mini-quizzes never tell me if *I* was right.** Ch1 says "Click an answer to reveal the right one below", then the answer starts "It's true —" no matter what I clicked. And only ch1 has an instruction; ch3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14 and 15 just show three buttons with nothing telling me to press one.

**19. Ch10's answer doesn't match its question.** Q is about a faked *voice*; the answer opens "Deepfake videos have fooled millions of people!" I thought I'd got someone else's answer.

**20. The gallery tells me to do it, then says I can't.** "Share it. Upload it below…" then "Please submit this on your child's behalf." No grown-up is here, so I don't know if I'm allowed to press the button. Also "make one with an AI image generator" argues with ch11's "Don't upload photos… without a parent's permission".

**21. "CVs" (ch15)** killed the best story in the book. "It was trained only on male engineers' CVs" — I guessed a CV was a kind of test, so I never understood *why* the AI was unfair.

**22. Ch5's lists are a run-on jumble.** "Perform quick mathematical calculation Find patterns in data Recognise faces from a crowd Predict the weather…" — no bullets or full stops, so I read "calculation Find" as one thing. Also "calculation" should be plural, "from a crowd" should be "in a crowd", and "Clean a person" sounds like giving someone a bath.

**23. Sayings I don't know:** "Think out of the box" (ch5), "Gartner Hype Cycle" (ch2), "Human-in-the-Loop" (ch15). Quote marks don't make a phrase understandable.

**24. "Ask your teacher to show you the graph!" (ch2).** I'm at home on a tablet. And the graph seems to already be there, so I thought there was a different one I was missing.

**25. The last line is spoken by nobody.** "If you use me safely, we can do miracles!" Who is "me"? It must be the AI — but ch11 just told me "AI is NOT a friend, it's a tool. It doesn't have feelings." "Miracles" made me think of church.

**26. "A walk through the six chapters of GR4AI"** — the menu says 01 to 15. I thought nine chapters were missing.

**27. Nallah never appears.** She's introduced as one of eight friends ("chef & storyteller") and never asks a question, while Katerina, Ronan and Fleur get two each. She should obviously have the food question in ch7 or ch14.

**28. Joke professor names.** "Prof. Goldilocks" and "Prof. Shy Jeans" in a section that says "Meet 9 amazing professionals". Goldilocks is a fairy tale about porridge; I couldn't tell if it was a joke, and if it is, I don't get it.

**29. Ch2's brain/computer lists don't line up.** Brain has five items, AI has four, so "Understands language" has no partner and I decided AI *can't* understand language — then ch8, 9 and 13 say it translates. Also "Gets tired & needs sleep" sits in a list of clever skills, as if being tired were a talent.

**30. The CO₂ fact lands out of nowhere (ch7)**, in the middle of Netflix and pizza robots. I don't know if 700,000 km is a lot, "CO₂" and "training a model" are unexplained, and ch9 says AI "Helps the Planet" — so which is it?

**31. Ch11's answer is news-speak.** "The European Union passed its landmark AI Act… It bans AI that manipulates children or uses secret surveillance." "Passed" made me think of passing a test. I got it right only because "A tax on robots" was silly.

**32. Ch13 jargon.** "UI/UX design" looks like a number plate. "Algebra & statistics" are words I haven't met in Year 5, so the tile meant to make maths exciting made it scary.

**33. "↕ Keep scrolling to shuffle through all 8 careers" (ch12).** "Shuffle" is what you do to cards — I thought scrolling would mix them up and I'd lose my place.

**34. The quiz rules made me nervous.** "the next question appears automatically" means I can't change my mind; not knowing my score for ten questions was annoying, not exciting. And a "Try again ↺" button sits next to "Question 1 of 10" before I've started.

**35. Maths that doesn't add up (ch8).** 0.1 seconds a scan should be hundreds of thousands a day, not "12,000 scans a day". I did it on paper and couldn't tell if the book or I was wrong.

**36. Too much SHOUTING.** "LOTS", "DATA", "ALWAYS", "ALL", "YOU", "NEVER" — nearly every paragraph. After a while the capitals stop meaning "important" and just feel like being shouted at.

**37. It swings between too babyish and too grown-up.** Ch3 has "Imagine teaching a toddler to recognise a cat" and, two paragraphs later, "parameters". Ch15 has "Your voice matters!" next to "non-negotiable".

## The bits I liked most (keep these)

The eight friends and their ages/hobbies. The chihuahua-and-blueberry-muffin joke (ch4) — best line in the book, I told my brother. "Just 5 days!" compared with Netflix and Instagram (ch4). The 42 days vs 10–15 years for the vaccine (ch9). Chapter 11's safety rules — short sentences, one rule per line, tells me exactly what to do; the clearest writing in the whole thing. "AI is NOT a friend, it's a tool." "Like flashcards!" and "Like levelling up in a game!" (ch3) — the only two comparisons that clicked instantly. Chapter 12's careers, especially "AI Game Designer" and "Study: Computing + Art + Maths", because it's about *my* future. "Curiosity and creativity… Sound like you?" (ch13) — nicest moment in the book. And the hiring story in ch15, apart from "CVs" — the biggest thing I learned and the one I could explain to someone else.
